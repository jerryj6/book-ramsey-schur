\title[$R(B_4,B_9)=27$]{The book Ramsey number $R(B_4,B_9)=27$}
\author{Jerry Yong (with computational assistance)}
\date{October 2026}
\thanks{Formal proof in Lean~4 with SAT certificates.}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
The book $B_n$ is $n$ triangles sharing one edge. We show
$R(B_4,B_9)=27$. Before this work the value was known only to lie in
$\{27,28\}$. The lower bound comes from the strongly regular graph
$\mathrm{srg}(26,10,3,4)$. For the upper bound, a degree-peeling
argument reduces the claim to $27$ SAT instances, each fixing the row
of one vertex of given red degree. Each instance is refuted by a
CaDiCaL LRAT certificate, $0.71$~GB of trimmed proof in all. The
whole argument is checked in Lean~4: Lean generates every CNF itself,
a Lean proof connects each CNF to the definition of a book-free
colouring, and Lean's verified LRAT checker accepts every certificate.
The resulting theorem
$\mathrm{LowerBound}\ 26\ 4\ 9 \wedge \mathrm{UpperBound}\ 27\ 4\ 9$
depends only on Lean's standard axioms and the \texttt{native\_decide}
compiler-trust axiom. drat-trim's \texttt{lrat-check} also accepts all
$27$ certificates independently.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For graphs $G$ and $H$, the Ramsey number $R(G,H)$ is the least $N$
such that every red/blue colouring of the edges of $K_N$ contains a
red $G$ or a blue $H$. Book Ramsey numbers were first studied
systematically by Rousseau and Sheehan \cite{RS78}, and they have
attracted renewed interest
\cite{CFW21,Wes24,LMPV24,DL26}. Books are also an ingredient in the
recent exponential improvement for diagonal Ramsey numbers. Rousseau
and Sheehan's counting bound gives $R(B_m,B_n)\le 2(m+n+1)$. For
$(m,n)=(4,9)$ this is $28$. A strongly regular graph gives the lower
bound $27$ (Section~3). Radziszowski's dynamic survey
\cite[Table~IXa]{Rad} lists $R(B_4,B_9)$ as $27$--$28$. The recent
computational papers \cite{LMPV24,Wes24} and the algebraic paper
\cite{DL26} do not treat this case.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
$R(B_4,B_9)=27$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is computer-assisted. Section~2 fixes notation, Section~3
gives the lower bound, Section~4 gives the reduction, Section~5 the
encoding, and Section~6 the certificates and their verification.
Section~7 lists the controls we ran against encoding errors.
Section~8 states exactly what has to be trusted.

\section{Preliminaries}

Identify a colouring of $K_N$ with its red graph $\cR$ on vertex set
$[N]$. For a pair $uv$, write $c_R(u,v)$ for the number of common red
neighbours and $c_B(u,v)$ for the number of common blue neighbours. A
red $B_m$ exists exactly when some red edge $uv$ has $c_R(u,v)\ge m$;
similarly a blue $B_n$ exists exactly when some blue edge has
$c_B(u,v)\ge n$. A colouring is $(m,n)$-good if it has neither. Then
$R(B_m,B_n)>N$ if and only if some $(m,n)$-good colouring of $K_N$
exists. The Lean development states this directly.
\texttt{Statement.lean} is 23 lines, uses no library beyond core Lean,
and is the only file a reader must audit to know what is proved:

\begin{lstlisting}
def BookFree (red : Fin N -> Fin N -> Bool) (m n : Nat) : Prop :=
  forall u v, u != v -> (red u v = true  -> redCodeg  red u v < m) /\
                        (red u v = false -> blueCodeg red u v < n)
def LowerBound N m n := exists red, Symm red /\ BookFree red m n
def UpperBound N m n := forall red, Symm red -> not BookFree red m n
\end{lstlisting}

Here \texttt{redCodeg} and \texttt{blueCodeg} count, by list filtering
over \texttt{Fin~N}, the vertices $w\notin\{u,v\}$ adjacent to both $u$
and $v$ in red (respectively, in blue). So Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
$\mathrm{LowerBound}\ 26\ 4\ 9 \wedge \mathrm{UpperBound}\ 27\ 4\ 9$.

\section{The lower bound}

Let $G$ be any $\mathrm{srg}(26,10,3,4)$, for example a Paulus graph.
Colour the edges of $G$ red and all other pairs blue. A red edge has
exactly $\lambda=3<4$ common red neighbours. Take a blue pair $uv$.
Exactly $\mu=4$ vertices are red-adjacent to both, so $2k-\mu=16$
vertices are red-adjacent to at least one of them. The common blue
neighbourhood therefore has size $24-16=8<9$. Hence the colouring is
$(4,9)$-good, and $R(B_4,B_9)\ge 27$. The Lean file
\texttt{B49Witness.lean} contains an explicit such graph (130 edges,
given in Appendix~A) and checks \texttt{BookFree} by
\texttt{native\_decide}. The graph was found by the SAT pipeline of
Section~5. Separately, a script independent of the CNF code confirms
that it is $10$-regular with $\lambda=3$ and $\mu=4$.

\section{Reduction by degree peeling}

Write $d(v)$ for the red degree. For $0\le lo\le d\le hi\le N-1$, let
$F(N,m,n,d,lo,hi)$ be the CNF of Section~5. It is satisfiable when an
$(m,n)$-good colouring of $K_N$ exists that has all red degrees in
$[lo,hi]$ and, after a suitable relabelling, has vertex $0$ of red
degree $d$.

\begin{lemma}[Row exclusion; Lean \texttt{row\_excl\_of\_check}]
\label{lem:rowexcl}
Suppose $F(N,m,n,d,lo,hi)$ is unsatisfiable. Then no $(m,n)$-good
colouring of $K_N$ with all red degrees in $[lo,hi]$ has a vertex of
red degree $d$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Suppose $c$ is such a colouring and $v$ has $d(v)=d$. Relabel so that
$v=0$ and its red neighbours are $1,\dots,d$. Next, apply the
permutation of $\{1,\dots,d\}$ and the permutation of
$\{d+1,\dots,N-1\}$ that make the colouring lexicographically maximal
among those fixing row $0$. The result satisfies every clause of $F$,
including the lex-leader clauses. Lean proves each step: relabelling
preserves \texttt{Symm}, \texttt{BookFree} and red degrees, and a
lex-maximal representative exists.
\end{proof}

Because $[lo',hi']\subseteq[lo,hi]$, an exclusion proved for $[lo,hi]$
also holds for every colouring whose degrees lie in $[lo',hi']$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:rowexcl} then lets us shrink the admissible degree
interval one endpoint at a time (\texttt{peel\_low},
\texttt{peel\_high}). Let $c$ be a $(4,9)$-good colouring of $K_{27}$.
Its degrees start in $[0,26]$, and the following refutations shrink
that range to nothing:

the parity argument is not used in Lean.

\subsection*{Remark: why the instances are small}

The following count explains why the instances are easy, and the
paper's other numbers do not depend on it. By Goodman's identity, the
number of monochromatic triangles is
$T=\binom{27}{3}-\frac12\sum_v d(v)(26-d(v))$. In a $(4,9)$-good
colouring each red edge lies in at most $3$ red triangles and each
blue edge in at most $8$ blue triangles. With $r$ red edges this gives
$T\le r+8(351-r)/3$. Combining the two, $\sum_v f(d(v))\ge 1989$,
where $f(d)=73d/6-d^2/2$. The maximum of $f$ is $f(12)=74$, so the
total deficit $\sum_v(74-f(d(v)))$ is at most $9$. The deficit is $0$
at $d=12$, $1/3$ at $d=13$, $2/3$ at $d=11$ and $7/3$ at $d=10$.
Counting alone therefore forces nearly all degrees into
$\{11,12,13\}$, and it excludes every colouring with all degrees in
$[8,10]$. Rousseau and Sheehan's bound of $28$ is the same count at
$N=28$. The substantive content of the certificates is that no vertex
can have red degree $11$, $12$ or $13$. Accordingly, the $d=11$
certificate is the largest (Table~2).

\section{Encoding}

Each pair $\{i,j\}$ gets one variable, true meaning red. Further
Tseitin variables $t_{R,ijk}$ and $t_{B,ijk}$ are shared by the three
pairs of each triple and mark red and blue triangles. For every pair
$\{i,j\}$, a sequential counter \cite{Sin05} over the $N-2$ red
triangle variables, guarded by $x_{ij}$, enforces at most $m-1$ of
them. A second counter over the blue triangle variables, guarded by
$\neg x_{ij}$, enforces at most $n-1$. Row~0 is fixed by unit clauses
($x_{0j}$ true exactly for $1\le j\le d$). Sequential counters bound
every red degree to $[lo,hi]$. Finally, lex-leader clauses order
consecutive rows within the blocks $\{1,\dots,d\}$ and
$\{d+1,\dots,N-1\}$. For $N=27$ the instances have
$122{,}958$--$124{,}362$ variables and $264{,}560$--$267{,}260$
clauses. The CNF is defined in Lean (\texttt{RowCNF.lean}, function
\texttt{rcnf}) as a pure function. The DIMACS files given to the
solver are printed by a compiled Lean executable from that same
definition, so the formula Lean checks is exactly the formula that was
solved.

\section{Certificates and verification}

CaDiCaL~3.0.1 produced each refutation as binary LRAT. Lean's
\texttt{lrattrim} trimmed it and gzip compressed it. Each certificate
is embedded in a Lean module as Base64 text. Lean decodes it with a
DEFLATE decoder written in pure Lean, parses it, and checks it against
\texttt{rcnf}\;$27\;4\;9\;d\;lo\;hi$ with the LRAT checker of Lean's
standard library (\texttt{Std.Tactic.BVDecide.LRAT.check}), whose
soundness is proved in Lean. The decoder's correctness is not part of
the trusted base: a faulty decoder could only produce a certificate
the checker rejects, never a false theorem. The checking computation
runs under \texttt{native\_decide}.

\subsection*{Independent check}

All $27$ trimmed certificates were also checked with
\texttt{lrat-check} from drat-trim \cite{WHH14}, against the same
DIMACS files. This checker shares no code with CaDiCaL or Lean, and it
accepted all $27$.

\paragraph{Released artifacts.} The claims above describe the original
verification campaign. The certificate archive released with this
paper was regenerated subsequently after a storage loss of the
original build tree; all $27$ \texttt{b49} monolithic certificates in
the archive again pass \texttt{lrat-check} against the released DIMACS
files, with per-file SHA-256 hashes recorded in
\texttt{docs/COVERAGE.csv} and the companion manifests. The Lean
source tree in the release is a partial reconstruction of the original
project — the modules it is missing, and the checker-verification
that stands in for them, are itemised in
\texttt{docs/LEAN\_STATUS.md}.

\section{Controls against encoding errors}

The instances are much easier than the neighbouring open case
$R(B_4,B_7)$ (Section~9), so we ran the following controls.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Satisfiable control at $N=26$: the same generator, with
$(d;lo,hi)=(10;10,10)$, returns SAT in $4.7$~s. The model is an
$\mathrm{srg}(26,10,3,4)$ and passes an independent codegree check.
Since the Section~4 remark makes $10$-regular colourings
Goodman-tight at $N=26$, this tests the encoding exactly at the
extremal configuration.
\item Independent generator: a separately written Python encoder with
the same semantics but different code agrees on the decisive cases at
$N=27$, $(8;8,10)$, $(10;9,10)$ and $(9;9,9)$, all UNSAT, and on the
$N=26$ SAT control.
\item Known values: the pipeline reproduces $R(B_3,B_5)=17$,
$R(B_2,B_9)=22$ and $R(B_4,B_6)=22$, with SAT on the corresponding
critical sizes. $R(B_4,B_6)\le 22$ is formalised end to end in the
same Lean framework.
\end{enumerate}
Controls (i)--(iii) are evidence for the encoding, not part of the
proof. The proof rests on Lemma~\ref{lem:rowexcl}, which Lean proves
against \texttt{Statement.lean}.

\section{Trusted base}

To accept Theorem~1 from the artefact, one must trust: the Lean~4
kernel (v4.34.1); the faithfulness of \texttt{Statement.lean} to the
mathematical definition (Section~2); and the Lean compiler and runtime
used by \texttt{native\_decide} for $28$ closed computations. One need
not trust CaDiCaL, the CNF printer, the DEFLATE decoder, the cube or
peeling scripts, or any Python code. The \texttt{native\_decide} trust
could be removed with a kernel-only \texttt{decide} on smaller
certificates, but that is impractical at $0.7$~GB. Section~6 records
the independent drat-trim check, which gives assurance that does not
depend on Lean's compiler.

\section{Related work and outlook}

The SAT approach to book Ramsey numbers follows \cite{Wes24}, and the
flag-algebra bounds are from \cite{LMPV24}. Combining row fixing and
degree peeling with a Lean-checked bridge to the definition is, as far
as we know, new for these problems. The same pipeline is being applied
to $R(B_4,B_7)\in\{22,23\}$. There every case of the analogous chain
has been refuted, and all $347$ certificates pass drat-trim's
checker, but the Lean assembly (tens of gigabytes of trimmed proof)
was still building when this paper was written. We make no claim about
$R(B_4,B_7)$ here. All negative attempts, including circulant and
block-circulant searches, Goodman cuts and monolithic solving, are
logged in the accompanying ledger.
