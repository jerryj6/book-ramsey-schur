\title[$R(B_4,B_7)=22$]{The book Ramsey number $R(B_4,B_7)=22$}
\author{Jerry Yong (with computational assistance)}
\date{October 2026}
\thanks{Formal proof in Lean~4 with SAT certificates.}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
The book $B_n$ is $n$ triangles sharing one edge. We prove
$R(B_4,B_7)=22$. This closes the gap $\{22,23\}$ left by Lidick\'y,
McKinley, Pfender and Van~Overberghe \cite{LMPV}, whose flag-algebra
bound gave $R(B_4,B_7)\le 23$. The lower bound is their $21$-vertex
colouring. For the upper bound, a degree-peeling argument reduces the
problem to $22$ row-fixed SAT instances. The two hardest are split
into $312$ and $13$ cubes, each with a certified coverage proof. All
$347$ LRAT certificates are checked inside Lean~4 against CNFs that
Lean itself generates. A Lean-proved semantic bridge connects these
CNFs to a statement file written from the definition. The final
theorem \texttt{R\_B4\_B7\_eq : LowerBound 21 4 7 $\wedge$ UpperBound
22 4 7} has no \texttt{sorry}. It depends only on Lean's standard
axioms and the trust in \texttt{native\_decide}. An independent LRAT
checker also accepted all $347$ certificates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

For graphs $G$ and $H$, $R(G,H)$ is the least $N$ such that every
red/blue colouring of the edges of $K_N$ contains a red $G$ or a blue
$H$. Small book Ramsey numbers are tabulated in Radziszowski's survey
\cite{Rad}, Table~IXa. The value $R(B_4,B_7)\in\{22,23\}$ was listed
as open in \cite{LMPV} and in the 2026 revision of \cite{Rad}. The
same pipeline settled $R(B_4,B_9)=27$ in a companion note \cite{B49}.
Note that $R(B_4,B_7)$ is not covered by the recent theorem
$R(B_{n-2},B_n)\le 4n-3$ of Dai and Lin \cite{DL26}, which addresses
books differing by two.

\begin{theorem}
$R(B_4,B_7)=22$.
\end{theorem}

A colouring of $K_N$ is $(m,n)$-good when every red edge has fewer
than $m$ common red neighbours and every blue edge has fewer than $n$
common blue neighbours. Thus $R(B_m,B_n)>N$ if and only if $K_N$ has
an $(m,n)$-good colouring. The Lean statement file (SHA-256
\texttt{88a1e1b3\dots d643}) defines this property directly by
counting codegrees, and states:

\begin{lstlisting}
def LowerBound N m n := exists red, Symm red /\ BookFree red m n
def UpperBound N m n := forall red, Symm red -> not BookFree red m n
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Lower bound}

LMPV \cite{LMPV} give a $(4,7)$-good colouring of $K_{21}$. We embed
it as a $21\times 21$ matrix and check it in Lean by evaluation
(\texttt{lmpv\_lower\_bound}).

\section{Reduction by degree peeling}

Let $F(d,lo,hi)$ be the CNF of Section~4. It asserts a $(4,7)$-good
colouring of $K_{22}$ with every red degree in $[lo,hi]$, in which
vertex~$0$ has red degree $d$, the red neighbours of vertex~$0$ are
$1,\dots,d$, and lex-leader constraints hold inside the blocks
$\{1,\dots,d\}$ and $\{d+1,\dots,21\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Row exclusion; Lean \texttt{row\_excl\_of\_check}]
If $F(d,lo,hi)$ is unsatisfiable, then no good colouring with all red
degrees in $[lo,hi]$ has a vertex of red degree $d$.
\end{lemma}

The proof relabels the vertex to $0$ and its red neighbours to
$1,\dots,d$. It then takes a lexicographically maximal representative
under the permutations of the two blocks, and that representative
satisfies the lex-leader constraints. Lean proves this once for all
$N,m,n,d,lo,hi$. The peeling chain below shrinks the admissible degree
interval until it is empty.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Peeling chain for $N=22$, $(m,n)=(4,7)$.}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
step & instances $(d;lo,hi)$ & degrees afterwards & certificate \\
\midrule
S1 & $d\in\{0..5,\,14..21\}$; $[0,21]$ & $[6,13]$ & monolithic \\

S3 & $(6;6,10)$ & $[7,10]$ & monolithic \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

We first tried the max-side case $(9;7,9)$. It produced a monolithic
proof above $6$~GiB, so we peeled from the minimum side instead. A
$9$-regular graph on $22$ vertices is not excluded by parity, so A3 is
a genuine case. It is refuted by its certificate.

\section{Encoding and cubes}

The encoding uses:
\begin{itemize}
\item one variable per edge;
\item Tseitin variables for red and blue triangles, shared by the
three edges of each triple;
\item sequential counters bounding the monochromatic codegree of each
edge, and the red degree of each vertex;
\item unit clauses fixing row~$0$, and the block lex-leader clauses.
\end{itemize}
Instances have $45$k--$59$k variables and $101$k--$127$k clauses. On
known values the encoding gives the expected answers: $(3,5)$ is SAT
at $16$ vertices and UNSAT at $17$, and $(4,6)$ is SAT at $21$ and
UNSAT at $22$. The same Lean pipeline reproves $R(B_4,B_6)\le 22$
(\texttt{R\_B4\_B6\_le\_22}).

Cases S4, A2 and A3 use cube-and-conquer. \texttt{march} generated the
cubes, and hard cubes were split again. Lean's
\texttt{unsat\_of\_cubes} states: if $F\wedge c$ is UNSAT for every
cube $c$, and $F\wedge\bigwedge_c\neg c$ is UNSAT, then $F$ is UNSAT.
The coverage formula is itself refuted by an LRAT certificate checked
in Lean.

\section{Certificates and verification}

Every instance was solved by CaDiCaL with direct binary LRAT output,
then trimmed with \texttt{lrattrim}. The trimmed text LRAT for S4
alone is about $40$~GB. In Lean, certificates are embedded as
base64(gzip(LRAT)) and decoded by a pure-Lean inflate. Soundness does
not depend on the decoder, because the only fact used is
\texttt{LRAT.check cert F = true} for the Lean-generated $F$. A faulty
decoder can only make a check fail. The S4 batches dominate the build:
$95$ modules, about $18$~h at two concurrent builds.

\texttt{\#print axioms R\_B4\_B7\_eq} lists \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}, plus one auxiliary
axiom per \texttt{native\_decide} computation. No \texttt{sorryAx}
appears. Separately, a C LRAT checker sharing no code with CaDiCaL or
Lean accepted all $347$ trimmed certificates.

During the final build, the compiled Lean outputs for $144$ S4 cube
proofs were lost after their text proofs had been deleted to save
disk. Those $144$ cubes were re-solved from the same Lean-generated
CNFs. The new proofs again passed the independent checker and Lean.
The manifest records both the original and the regenerated hashes.

\section{Negative results}

The following attempts did not succeed:
\begin{itemize}
\item exhaustive circulant and 2-block-circulant searches on $22$
vertices, and searches with six prescribed automorphism groups, found
no good colouring;
\item one-vertex extensions of the LMPV witness all failed;
\item Goodman-count cuts gave no speedup;
\item monolithic Kissat and CaDiCaL runs on S4 timed out at $6$~h;
\item unrestricted cube-and-conquer on the full problem ($546$ cubes)
stalled on a hard block.
\end{itemize}
A complete log of attempts is kept with the artifacts.

\section{Scope}

This settles one table entry. As with $R(B_4,B_9)$, the methodological
point is the end-to-end Lean chain from the raw definition, and its
scaling to tens of gigabytes of proof text. We checked arXiv through
October~2026 and found no other proof. We cannot exclude unpublished
work.
